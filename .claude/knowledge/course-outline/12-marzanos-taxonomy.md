# Marzano's New Taxonomy (Marzano & Kendall, 2001/2007)

## Origin

Robert J. Marzano proposed *A New Taxonomy of Educational Objectives* in 2000 (Corwin Press), revised with John Kendall in *The New Taxonomy of Educational Objectives* (2007). The framework builds on Bloom's taxonomy but argues that Bloom's cognitive hierarchy is incomplete because it ignores the systems that **decide whether to engage in thinking** in the first place.

Marzano's central argument: teaching cognitive strategies is insufficient if learners are not motivated to use them, and motivation is governed not by cognition but by the **self-system** — the learner's beliefs about importance, self-efficacy, and emotional response to a task. You cannot activate the cognitive system without first clearing the self-system's engagement decision. This makes Marzano's taxonomy particularly relevant for corporate learning, where motivation, engagement, and self-regulation are as important as cognitive processing.

> Marzano (2001): "The self-system makes the initial decision about whether to engage in a new task — if that decision is negative, no learning occurs regardless of how well the cognitive content is designed."

---

## The two dimensions

### Dimension 1: Three Knowledge Domains

All learning content falls into one of three knowledge domains. The taxonomy applies to all three:

| Domain | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Information** (Declarative Knowledge) | The "what" — facts, concepts, generalizations, principles | Definitions, frameworks, cause-effect relationships, theories |
| **Mental Procedures** (Procedural Knowledge) | The "how-to" — processes, skills, tactics, algorithms | Using a planning tool, running a root cause analysis, applying a formula |
| **Psychomotor Procedures** | Physical performance skills combining mental and motor execution | Operating equipment, data entry fluency, presenting while managing slides |

### Dimension 2: Three Systems of Processing (+ Six Levels)

The three systems engage in sequence — from the top down. The Self-System decides first; if it gives a green light, the Metacognitive System sets goals; then the Cognitive System processes.

---

## The three systems

### System 1 — Self-System (highest in sequence)

The Self-System makes the initial decision about whether to engage in a new task. It does this by evaluating:

- **Importance** — Is this task relevant to my goals, values, or identity?
- **Self-efficacy** — Do I believe I am capable of succeeding at this?
- **Emotional response** — What emotions does this task activate in me?
- **Overall motivation** — Do I want to do this?

If the Self-System decides not to engage, no amount of well-designed cognitive content will produce learning. The learner may be physically present but cognitively absent.

**Design implications:**
- Always make the relevance of content explicit and personal — connect to the learner's role, goals, and real work situations
- Build self-efficacy through graduated challenge — early success experiences reduce threat responses
- Reduce emotional threat by normalizing difficulty ("most learners need several practice attempts before this clicks")
- Activation order: before launching cognitive instruction, check whether the Self-System has been addressed

**Marzano's self-system strategies:**
- Examining importance: "Here is why this matters to your specific role as [X]"
- Examining self-efficacy: scaffold tasks from achievable → challenging
- Examining emotional response: acknowledge that the topic may feel threatening or irrelevant, and address that head-on

---

### System 2 — Metacognitive System

Once the Self-System has engaged, the Metacognitive System acts as executive control — setting goals, monitoring progress, and adjusting strategy.

**Four metacognitive processes:**
1. **Specifying goals** — What am I trying to achieve? What does success look like?
2. **Process monitoring** — Is my current approach working? Am I making progress?
3. **Monitoring clarity** — Do I actually understand this, or do I just think I do?
4. **Monitoring accuracy** — Are the conclusions I'm drawing correct?

**Design implications:**
- Include explicit goal-setting prompts before complex learning tasks ("Before you start, write down what you expect to learn and what success looks like for you")
- Build in reflection checkpoints during extended learning activities
- Design knowledge checks that reveal whether learners can accurately assess their own understanding (metacognitive awareness)
- Include self-assessment rubrics so learners can monitor their own process

**Note on Bloom's comparison:** Anderson & Krathwohl's revision (2001) included Metacognitive Knowledge as a passive dimension — something to be learned about, not an active system controlling cognition. Marzano treats metacognition as an active, higher-order system that operates above and across all cognitive processing. Research supports this view: Marzano cites an effect size of 0.56 for teaching goal-setting strategies and 0.69 for teaching metacognitive strategies on student achievement (*Classroom Instruction That Works*, 2001).

---

### System 3 — Cognitive System

Once the Self and Metacognitive systems have cleared the path, the Cognitive System processes knowledge. Marzano identifies four cognitive levels within this system, ordered by processing demand:

#### Level 1 — Knowledge Retrieval
Recognizing, recalling, or executing information from memory. Equivalent to Bloom's Remember.

- Recognition (matching a stimulus to memory)
- Recall (retrieving specific information)
- Execution (performing a familiar procedure)

#### Level 2 — Comprehension
Translating knowledge into an internal representation — identifying what is important and how it is structured. Goes beyond pure recall; requires synthesis and symbolic representation.

- **Synthesis:** Identify the most important elements; delete what is extraneous
- **Representation:** Construct a non-linguistic or symbolic model of the knowledge

> Note: Marzano's Comprehension is more active than Bloom's Understand — it involves transforming information into a mental model, not just restating it.

#### Level 3 — Analysis
Extending knowledge by generating new inferences, identifying errors, or constructing generalizations. Four analytical processes:

- **Matching:** Identifying similarities and differences
- **Classifying:** Grouping items into categories based on attributes
- **Error analysis:** Identifying logical errors, faulty reasoning, or irrelevant information
- **Generalizing:** Constructing principles from specific cases
- **Specifying:** Applying a principle to generate a specific prediction or application

#### Level 4 — Knowledge Utilization
Using knowledge to accomplish a specific task in the real world. The highest-demand cognitive level; requires all lower levels.

- **Decision making:** Selecting among alternatives when no single option is clearly best
- **Problem solving:** Achieving a goal when an obstacle exists
- **Experimenting:** Generating and testing hypotheses about a phenomenon
- **Investigating:** Examining a past, present, or future situation to draw conclusions

> Knowledge Utilization maps most closely to Bloom's Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, and Create — but Marzano's version emphasizes real-world task performance, not just cognitive exercise.

## The engagement sequence in practice

Marzano's taxonomy has a **top-down engagement sequence** that differs from Bloom's bottom-up hierarchy:

```
Self-System decides to engage
        ↓
Metacognitive System sets goals and monitors
        ↓
Cognitive System processes:
  Level 1: Retrieval
  Level 2: Comprehension
  Level 3: Analysis
  Level 4: Knowledge Utilization
```

This means: address motivation and relevance first, build metacognitive awareness second, then develop cognitive capability from retrieval upward.

## Marzano's taxonomy applied to learning design

### Diagnosis before design

Before designing any module, run this diagnostic:

1. **Self-System check:** Do learners in this role currently find this topic relevant and feel capable of succeeding? If no → design for motivation, relevance, and psychological safety first.
2. **Metacognitive check:** Are learners aware of what they know and don't know about this topic? If no → include self-assessment and goal-setting activities.
3. **Cognitive level:** What cognitive demand does the job requirement actually need — retrieval, comprehension, analysis, or utilization?

### Designing for disengaged learners

Bloom's taxonomy assumes learners are ready to engage. Marzano's is useful precisely when they are not. When designing for:

- **Learners who don't see the point:** Address Self-System / Importance before delivering content
- **Learners with low confidence:** Address Self-System / Self-Efficacy with scaffolded early wins
- **Learners who don't know what they don't know:** Address Metacognitive / Monitoring Clarity with diagnostic self-assessments
- **Learners who know facts but can't apply them:** Address Cognitive Level 4 / Knowledge Utilization with decision-making scenarios

### Action verbs by cognitive level

| Level | Verbs |
|---|---|
| Retrieval (L1) | list, name, identify, recall, recognize, state, reproduce, execute |
| Comprehension (L2) | explain, summarize, paraphrase, represent, model, symbolize, illustrate |
| Analysis (L3) | compare, contrast, classify, categorize, analyze errors, generalize, specify, infer |
| Knowledge Utilization (L4) | decide, solve, investigate, experiment, design, hypothesize, produce, perform |
| Metacognitive | set goals, monitor, self-assess, reflect, adjust strategy, evaluate accuracy |
| Self-System | evaluate importance, examine self-efficacy, manage emotional response, commit |

## Comparison to Bloom's revised taxonomy

| Dimension | Bloom's Revised (2001) | Marzano's New Taxonomy (2001) |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage | Cognitive processes only | Cognitive + Metacognitive + Self-System (motivation) |
| Metacognition | Passive knowledge type | Active processing system above cognition |
| Motivation | Not addressed | Central (Self-System is the first gate) |
| Hierarchy direction | Bottom-up (Remember → Create) | Top-down engagement, then bottom-up cognition |
| Best for | Objective writing, cognitive audit | Motivation-aware design, self-regulation, disengaged learners |
| Research base | Widely validated | Strong metacognitive research base; less adopted in practice than Bloom's |

---

## Quick reference

- Three systems: **Self-System (motivation gate) → Metacognitive System (executive control) → Cognitive System (processing)**
- Cognitive levels: **Retrieval → Comprehension → Analysis → Knowledge Utilization**
- Three knowledge domains: **Information (declarative), Mental Procedures, Psychomotor Procedures**
- Engagement is **top-down**: self before metacognition before cognition
- Use when **motivation, self-efficacy, or self-regulation** are design constraints — Bloom's cannot address these
- Metacognitive teaching produces effect sizes of 0.56–0.69 on achievement
- Best for: disengaged learners, complex skill development, professional development programs where self-direction matters
