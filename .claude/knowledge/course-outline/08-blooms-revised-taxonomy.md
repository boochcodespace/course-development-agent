# Bloom's Revised Taxonomy (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001)

## Origin and revision history

Benjamin Bloom and collaborators published the original *Taxonomy of Educational Objectives: Handbook I, Cognitive Domain* in 1956. The framework organized educational goals into six hierarchical categories expressed as nouns: Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis, and Evaluation.

In 2001, Lorin Anderson and David Krathwohl led a revision that addressed six decades of accumulated critique and cognitive science research. The revised version, published as *A Taxonomy for Learning, Teaching, and Assessing* (Anderson, Krathwohl et al., 2001), made four structural changes:

1. Levels were renamed as **verbs** (gerunds), reflecting the active nature of cognition rather than static categories.
2. **Synthesis and Evaluation swapped positions** — Create now sits at the apex, above Evaluate.
3. A new **Knowledge Dimension** was added as an orthogonal axis: Factual, Conceptual, Procedural, and Metacognitive knowledge.
4. **Metacognition** was explicitly included as a knowledge type, though critics (including Marzano) argue it was still treated too passively.

> **Krathwohl's 2002 summary** (*Theory into Practice*, 41(4)) is the most commonly cited accessible overview. The full taxonomy table is two-dimensional: cognitive processes on one axis, knowledge types on the other.

## The two dimensions

### Cognitive Process Dimension (6 levels)

| Level | Core process | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Remember** | Retrieve | Recall or recognize relevant knowledge from memory |
| **2. Understand** | Construct meaning | Interpret, classify, summarize, infer, compare, explain |
| **3. Apply** | Use knowledge | Execute or implement a procedure in a given situation |
| **4. Analyze** | Break down | Differentiate, organize, and attribute components and relationships |
| **5. Evaluate** | Make judgments | Critique and check based on criteria and standards |
| **6. Create** | Put together | Generate, plan, and produce something new |

The levels are cumulative — higher levels incorporate lower ones. A learner who cannot Remember cannot reliably Apply. However, verb alone does not determine level; context and cognitive demand matter (Crowe et al., 2008 note that "describe" can appear at Remember or Create depending on task design).

### Knowledge Dimension (4 types)

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Factual** | Basic elements — terminology, specific details and elements |
| **Conceptual** | Interrelationships — classifications, principles, theories, models |
| **Procedural** | How to do something — skills, algorithms, techniques, methods |
| **Metacognitive** | Knowledge of one's own cognition and learning strategies |

**The Taxonomy Table** plots any learning objective at the intersection of a cognitive process level and a knowledge type. Example: "Explain how to apply the rule of 72" sits at Understand × Procedural.

## Action verb banks (revised taxonomy)

Use these to write Bloom's-aligned learning objectives. The verb signals the cognitive demand.

- **Remember** — define, identify, list, name, recall, recognize, reproduce, state, label, match, cite, locate, memorize
- **Understand** — classify, describe, explain, paraphrase, summarize, compare, contrast, illustrate, infer, interpret, restate, translate, discuss, distinguish
- **Apply** — apply, demonstrate, employ, execute, implement, operate, solve, use, practice, compute, modify, construct, simulate, carry out
- **Analyze** — analyze, categorize, compare, contrast, deconstruct, differentiate, examine, organize, outline, diagram, attribute, distinguish, break down
- **Evaluate** — appraise, assess, critique, defend, judge, justify, prioritize, recommend, validate, argue, check, monitor, conclude, rate
- **Create** — compose, construct, design, develop, devise, formulate, generate, hypothesize, invent, plan, produce, synthesize, assemble, author

> **Design Tip — Verb drift:** The most common AI content defect. Objective says "Apply" but the knowledge check only asks "What is the definition of X?" Tag every objective with its Bloom level and confirm that all associated activities and assessment items require the same level of processing, not a lower one.

## What Bloom's revised taxonomy does well

- Provides a shared language for writing and auditing learning objectives across teams and organizations
- Guides progression from surface knowledge to higher-order application in multi-module programs
- Works as an alignment tool: objective → activity → assessment, all at the same cognitive level
- The two-dimensional table surfaces gaps — if all objectives cluster in the Factual × Remember cell, the course is shallow
- Works across domains (technical, soft skills, compliance, leadership)

---

## What Bloom's revised taxonomy does NOT do

- It addresses **cognitive processes only**. It does not account for motivation, emotional engagement, metacognitive self-regulation as an active process, or affective dimensions of learning. For those, use Fink's Taxonomy, Marzano's Taxonomy, or SDT.
- It describes **types of cognition**, not the **depth of complexity** required within a task. For that, use Webb's Depth of Knowledge.
- It describes **structural categories of understanding**, not the **quality and integration** of a learner's response. For that, use SOLO Taxonomy.
- It is a **hierarchical model** — some critics argue the strict ordering breaks down in practice (analysis does not always require application as a prerequisite, depending on domain).

## Bloom's revised taxonomy applied to learning design

### Objective writing formula

**[Bloom's verb] + [object] + [context/condition]**

- "Identify the three components of a supply chain risk assessment given a scenario with incomplete data" (Apply × Procedural)
- "Design a re-order point calculation for a seasonal product using given demand and lead time data" (Create × Procedural)
- "Evaluate the trade-offs between service level and inventory cost for two competing planning strategies" (Evaluate × Conceptual)

### Course arc progression

Foundational courses typically target Remember and Understand in early modules, progress to Apply in mid-modules, and reach Analyze or Evaluate in capstone activities. Reaching Create in a single short course is rare; it is most appropriate for practitioner-level or advanced certification programs.

### Assessment alignment by level

| Bloom's Level | Appropriate assessment types |
|---|---|
| Remember | MCQ, matching, fill-in-the-blank, labeling |
| Understand | Short answer, paraphrase exercise, classification task |
| Apply | Scenario-based MCQ, lab exercise, simulation task |
| Analyze | Case study analysis, data interpretation, error-finding task |
| Evaluate | Critique exercise, decision justification, peer review rubric |
| Create | Project-based assessment, design task, capstone |

## Comparison to other taxonomies

| Dimension | Bloom's Revised | Fink's | Webb's DOK | SOLO | Marzano's |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Focus | Cognitive process type | Breadth of significant learning | Depth of cognitive complexity | Structural complexity of understanding | Self + metacognitive + cognitive systems |
| Hierarchy | Yes (cumulative) | No (interactive) | Yes (complexity) | Yes (depth) | Yes (engagement sequence) |
| Includes affect/motivation | No | Yes (Caring, Human Dimension) | No | No | Yes (Self-System) |
| Best for | Writing objectives, auditing cognitive level | Holistic course design, affective goals | Assessment rigor, task complexity | Formative feedback, response quality | Motivation-aware design |

## Quick reference

- Revised Bloom's uses **verbs** — Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create.
- It is **two-dimensional**: cognitive process × knowledge type (Factual, Conceptual, Procedural, Metacognitive).
- Use it to **write and audit** learning objectives and assessment alignment.
- It is **cognitive only** — pair with Fink, Marzano, or SDT when affective or self-regulation dimensions matter.
- Watch for **verb drift**: the single most common instructional design error when working with AI-generated content.
